<h2> On the Lamb! – GMTK 2025 Game Jam </h2>

<p>
In July 2025, I teamed up with four other developers to make On the Lamb! for the GMTK Game Jam. Game jams are all about speed — you get just a few days to go from concept to finished game. This jam’s theme was “loop”, so we built a game where you literally throw loops with your lasso to round up sheep.
</p>

<p>
Here’s the short description from our game page:

  _In On The Lamb! you play as an engineer-turned-rancher in exile with an eldritch being in their backyard. Lasso sheep to add to your herd and use their wool to build wacky machines that unlock new horizons for all sheepkind—maybe even a chance to escape the living nightmare you've found yourself in._
  </p>

---

## My Role

I was the head programmer, and most of my work revolved around building the systems that made the game actually feel alive:

Sheep movement & flocking – I wrote the code that drives thousands of sheep roaming around the fields. Since that many entities can crush performance, I spent a lot of time on optimization, making sure it stayed playable.

Tools – The lasso and shears were my big contributions. You can throw the lasso to loop sheep and drag them around, or shear a sheep to make wool pop off while it bolts away.

Inventory system – I built the system for equipping tools, picking up wool and sheep, and upgrading equipment. The inventory also tracks wool colors so they match the sheep they came from.

Pen system – Once a sheep is caught, it moves into pens automatically.

## Results & Reception

Out of ~9,500 entries, here’s where On the Lamb! placed across categories:

339 – Narrative
654 – Audio
852 – Artwork
1141 – Creativity
1685 – Enjoyment

Not bad for four frantic days of coding and bug-hunting!

Of course, no jam is perfect — we had a couple of game-breaking bugs sneak into the release build. I actually fixed them an hour after the deadline, but game jam rules mean you can’t update once submissions close. Lesson learned.

## What I Learned

This project taught me how to rapidly prototype complex systems under tight deadlines. Our team had to brainstorm ideas quickly, split responsibilities, and iterate on features until they were playable. It was chaotic, but I came away with sharper programming skills and a better sense of how to balance performance, design, and fun.
